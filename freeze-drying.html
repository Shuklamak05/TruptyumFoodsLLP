Freeze-drying/ Lyophilization

Freeze-drying, also known as lyophilization or vacuum sublimation, is a dehydration process used to preserve foods, ingredients, and other heat-sensitive products. In this method, the product is first frozen and then placed in a vacuum system where the water is removed through sublimation—directly converting the frozen water into vapor. This technique maintains the product’s structure and properties.

Why Freeze Drying?

Nutrient Preservation

Retains essential vitamins, minerals, ensuring maximum nutritional value.

Flavour and Texture Integrity

Maintains natural taste and texture, delivering a fresh, unaltered product.

Extended Shelf Life

Keeps products stable for months or years without refrigeration.

Lightweight Convenience

Makes transportation and storage easy, thanks to reduced weight.

Efficient Rehydration

Quickly restores products to their original state with water, retaining quality.

The Freeze-Drying Process Step-by-Step

The lyophilization process begins with fresh products at room temperature.

1

Freezing

Products are rapidly frozen to -40°C, locking in their structure and nutrients.

2

Sublimation

Under vacuum, gentle heating causes the ice to transition directly from solid to vapour, separating from the product.

3

Finalization

Atmospheric pressure is restored, and the freeze-dried product is extracted and vacuum-packed for preservation.
