Frequently Asked Questions

Find quick answers about freeze-drying (lyophilization) technology, contract manufacturing, and bulk order processes.

Freeze-drying, or lyophilization, is a high-tech dehydration process. It works by freezing the product (fruits, vegetables, herbs, or meals) to sub-zero temperatures (usually -40°C), reducing pressure, and then adding heat to allow the frozen water in the material to transition directly from solid ice to water vapor through sublimation. This leaves the cellular structure intact, preserving texture, flavor, and nutrients.

Unlike standard hot-air dehydration which shrinks food and destroys delicate heat-sensitive nutrients, freeze-drying retains up to 97% of nutritional value, preserves original shape and color, enables instant rehydration in water, and yields a lightweight product with an exceptionally long shelf life without using additives.

When stored in proper moisture-barrier and airtight oxygen-absorber packaging, freeze-dried products can have a shelf life of 25+ years. For commercial retail packs, they typically carry a shelf life of 12 to 24 months, depending on moisture exposure and packaging integrity.
